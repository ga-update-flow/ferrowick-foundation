Collective Impact

One of Aquidneck Island’s great strengths is its willingness to come together across town lines to solve problems. Building on that tradition, the Foundation was an early supporter of the collective impact framework described by John Kania and Mark Kramer in their 2011 Stanford Social Innovation Review article, “Collective Impact.”

Collective impact is a disciplined form of cross-sector collaboration. Public agencies, businesses, nonprofits and residents commit to a common agenda for solving a complex problem that no single organization could solve alone. Participants form a steering committee and working groups and pursue population-level change together.

Several conditions are generally considered essential to collective impact:

  • Common agenda: Participants share a vision for change, a common understanding of the problem and an agreed approach to solving it.
  • Shared measurement: Data are collected and results measured the same way across all participants, keeping efforts aligned and partners accountable to one another.
  • Mutually reinforcing activities: Each participant does what it does best, coordinated through a shared plan of action.
  • Continuous communication: Frequent, open communication builds trust and keeps objectives aligned.
  • Backbone support: A dedicated organization with its own staff coordinates the initiative and its partners.

These conditions are strongest when the following principles of practice are in place:

  • Design and carry out the initiative with a priority on equity.
  • Include community members in the collaboration.
  • Recruit and co-create with partners from every sector.
  • Use data to continuously learn, adapt and improve.
  • Develop leaders with system leadership skills.
  • Focus on program and system strategies.
  • Build a culture of relationships, trust and respect.
  • Customize for local context.

The Foundation convened and seed-funded its first collective impact initiative in 2012: the Island Youth Prevention Coalition, which brought schools, police, health providers and parents together to reduce underage drinking and vaping.

Coalition partners went on to achieve measurable results, including a sustained drop in self-reported youth alcohol use and the adoption of consistent prevention curricula in every island middle school. The coalition now receives state and federal support.

The Foundation continues to champion collective impact as a practical approach to community problem-solving. We have helped launch or sustain several initiatives addressing a range of island priorities, including:

  • Island Youth Prevention Coalition
  • Aquidneck Maternal and Infant Health Network
  • North End Early Learning Partnership
  • Island Child Wellness Collaborative
  • Pathways to Work Partnership
  • Equity Roundtable of Aquidneck Island

The Ferrowick Foundation will continue to support the goals of these initiatives. We invite current and prospective grantees to consider how their work connects to them and to talk with us about how we might strengthen that work together.

To learn more about collective impact and the Foundation’s approach, see:

  • “Collective Impact,” by John Kania & Mark Kramer, Stanford Social Innovation Review
  • The Foundation’s “Collective Impact Proposal Guidance”: for programs or projects that advance collective impact efforts on Aquidneck Island.
  • “Getting Started,” resources from the Collective Impact Forum
